How many sites can one robot really serve?

The deck says one fleet serves 6–10 sites, and that the robot leaves "its dock" with video in about 2 minutes. A ground robot can't do both: it can't drive between substations kilometres apart in two minutes. This model shows the trade-off so you can explain it with numbers instead of conceding it with words.

How the model works

One robot is a single-server queue. Cues arrive at random (Poisson) at rate λ = sites × cues per site per day. Each mission occupies the robot for S = launch (2 min) + travel out + time on site + travel back + re-dock. Utilisation is ρ = λ·S. With fixed mission times the mean wait in queue is ρ·S / (2(1 − ρ)) (Pollaczek–Khinchine), so waits explode as ρ approaches 1. At ρ ≥ 1 the queue grows forever.

The Monte Carlo run draws 30 days of cues, varies each site's distance by ±40% around the mean, and serves cues first come, first served. It reports what the formula cannot: the 90th percentile and the worst case.
